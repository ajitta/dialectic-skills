# dia-techne: where the method and the name come from

The rules are in SKILL.md; this file says what they rest on and where the skill departs from its source. Quotations are from the sources listed at the end.

## 1. The method: Rieseberg's tutorial

Felix Rieseberg's "How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models" (13 October 2026) sorts ambition into four kinds: "Speed, Altitude, Depth, and Range". It skips speed as well established.

- **Altitude:** "considering the outcome rather than the individual task". The tutorial limits it: it "works best if you really are after some ultimate outcome; it works less well for creative endeavors."
- **Depth:** work on a task "the way a field-leading expert would", touching "the underlying data, tools, and problems". Its depth prompt is the source of the skill's reading the material in its rawest form, of the first two questions in The work, and of the outside judge of its check: "I want a deeper version of this task, not a bigger one. Before rewriting it: (1) say what a top professional in this field would do here that I'm not asking for; (2) name the rawest material they would start from instead of what has already been digested for me; (3) name the specific methods and tools they would use; (4) say what, other than you or me, would show the result is right. Then rewrite my prompt to ask for exactly that."
- **Range:** draw on other fields, turn nouns into verbs ("A quarterly review isn't 'a deck'. It's 'getting busy people to believe a story about cause and effect, and to decide under uncertainty, in 30 minutes'"), and bring in opposing roles. This is the last question in The work.
- **Outside judge:** "Do not ask the model to judge itself. … insist on outside validation. Ideally that's a tool the model can run to get feedback immediately."
- **No role-play:** "models often switch vocabulary without actually switching methods"; "Don't ask the model to write like a short seller, ask it to do what a short seller does."
- **Challenge what you know:** ask the model "to adversarially check assumptions" against the raw data.

Not taken by name: "Challenge what you know" (above), "Pick unreasonable goals" and "Produce new knowledge" (showing a model predicts on data it has not seen) are depth techniques the skill does not ask for by name.

## 2. What the skill adds

These are not in the tutorial, which is written for a person improving their own request:

- The skill does the work itself rather than rewriting the request, and writes a prompt only when the user wants the work done by another model or agent; it sets the thing asked for beside at least one other way and takes the one the outcome favours.
- "Deeper not bigger" is read as leaving out what adds volume without changing the result.
- Recomputing figures from final values came from three small pilot runs.

## 3. The name

The name borrows Aristotle's *technē* as a label only. In *Nicomachean Ethics* VI.4 technē is a state of making that involves true reasoning, about things whose origin is in the maker; *Metaphysics* I.1 adds that the person of art knows the why, not only the that. Making, unlike action, has an end beyond itself (NE VI.5). The skill's start from the outcome a request serves fits that distinction, but it comes from Rieseberg's altitude. The skill does not implement an Aristotelian procedure; its method is Rieseberg's.
