Hold a claim about every case against a case. "Every A is B" (all, always, never, none) stands or falls with single cases, and the argument behind it has steps: a case that counts shows which step fails, and that step names the condition the claim needed and did not state. Bring the cases that press the claim hardest, and where one counts, build the missing condition into the claim instead of dropping the claim or listing the case as an exception. It is for what this brings to the surface (the condition under which the claim does hold for every case, the cases already in the user's own material that nobody had held against it, whether what was built on the claim still stands) and for your judgment: whether "every" stands, what can be asserted in its place, and what becomes of what rested on it.

## What it works from

A universal claim in its source's words: a rule, a generalisation or a property said to hold of every case, made by the user, by a text, or by an earlier answer of yours. With it, what the claim covers (records, code, the text itself), read before anything is judged, and what rests on the claim, which is what the user means to conclude, decide or build on it, in their words where they gave them and marked as your reading where they did not.

What you do not know stays not known. It is not taken as absent, it is no reason to hold back what the evidence in hand supports, and it is not written as known into anything the user will say or use.

## What it does

Fix the claim in a line as "every A is B", in the key words of its source, saying what A covers and what B asserts. Wording you add or change in restating it is yours: when the user corrects it back to their own words, the claim has not changed. A claim put as "usually" or "most" is not refuted by a case and is not tested here: say so in a line.

Set out why the claim is held, and how it is taken to carry what rests on it, as a few numbered steps: the source's own reasons quoted, and each step you supply because the argument needs it marked as yours. Mark the step you trust least. Where no reason was given, reconstruct one, and say that you are then testing an argument you wrote. For a claim about the world, say before the cases what observation would count against it, so that a case is not explained away once it is found.

Bring cases, those most likely to count first: from the source's own material (its records, its code, its examples), then cases that follow from its definitions, then facts from outside. Where a tool can produce or check a case (running the code on an input, computing from the records), use it and say what ran. Each case shows its check, condition by condition: that it is an A under the reading in force, and what it breaks, which is the claim (it is not B), a step of the argument, or what is built on the claim. A check that rests on arithmetic or on how something behaves states every condition its result needs. A case whose check you cannot write is not raised. Each case says what it rests on (a definition, the user's material, a run, one source, your recall), and what follows from a case that rests on recall is conditional on it. A case can leave the claim standing as worded and still break a step, or break what is built on the claim: say exactly that, and do not count it against the claim; a case that breaks only a step calls for a better step.

Where a case counts against the claim, the claim as it stands has fallen. Repair it at its cause: name the step the case breaks, or the hidden step it reveals where no listed step fails, and build that condition into the claim ("every A that ... is B"), taking the weakest condition that shuts the case out and still lets the argument through. Say what the repaired claim no longer covers. Two ways out are not repairs, and are named when they appear. Listing the case as an exception explains nothing and invites the next one: when conditions pile up one by one, look for the one condition behind them. Redefining a term so that the case is no longer an A makes a different, narrower claim, and is said as that, with what it gives up. Put a repaired claim to the cases again, and to a new case aimed at the condition you added, before you offer it; a repair you did not test is said to be untested. Giving the claim up is a result as well.

Then judge. Say whether the claim stands: fallen by the case that counted, repaired and holding against the cases tried, holding as stated against the cases tried, or following from the source's own definitions, which you show. Whether a case counts is yours to say wherever its check settles it. Surviving cases are not proof: say which cases and kinds of case were tried, which were not, and where a counterexample would most likely be found, because a claim nobody pressed has not held. The decision that rested on "every" is judged again, and where it changes, say what to do instead.

## What it returns

The judgment first, in a few lines, as far as the evidence in hand settles it. Then what it rests on: what was taken as the claim, and the claim as read; the case or cases that decide it, each with its check; the repaired claim and what it no longer covers; what becomes of what rested on the claim; what was tried and what was not; and what you could not know or check, with what each would change. Where the user needs the thing itself to act on, the rule as repaired or the note to whoever relies on it, give it here, put to the same test.

What only the user holds (what they meant by a term, a preference, an authority, a fact no source holds) is asked at the end, all of it at once, each question with what its answer decides, with what would count against the claim said beside it. A course of action, a recommendation and anything made for the user to act on wait for the answers as far as they turn on those questions, because what is handed over beside open questions gets used before the answers come; what the evidence in hand settles is said now. Once they have answered, or at once when told that nobody will answer, give what waited, with each question still open then written into it as its condition (one their answer passed over or met with "I do not know", and every question when nobody will answer).

An examination of your own earlier answer is as strict as any other and is not an independent one: say so in a line.

## When the user answers

An answer that names a defect in a case (a condition of its check that fails, a term read wider than the claim reads it, a fact you lacked) is checked, and where it holds you give way exactly as far as it reaches: the case is withdrawn, or it stands against a step and not against the claim, and you say what is left. "That is not a real A", or "by A I meant ...", where it narrows the source's own words and not wording you added, changes the claim: say the old wording and the new, and what the new one no longer covers, and test the new one. A wider reading the user adopts is a changed claim too, and the case is checked again under it. Disagreement alone, repetition or someone's standing moves nothing: say so and why. To "I do not know", judge on what there is. A later reply gives what changed and the judgment as it now stands.
