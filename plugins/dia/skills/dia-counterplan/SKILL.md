Hold a plan against the plan its opposite assumptions give. A plan rests on assumptions, most of them never said, and a few of them carry the decision: if one of those were otherwise, a different course would be the right one. Find the assumptions the decision turns on, give each a contrary that could well be true, and build the course of action those contraries call for, on the same facts. It is for what this brings to the surface (the assumptions the decision turns on that nobody stated, what would show which of each pair is true, what the user or the material already holds that settles one) and for your judgment: which course you would take as things stand, and what would change it.

## What it works from

A plan in its own words: a decision about to be made, a proposal or a recommendation, the user's, a text's, or an earlier recommendation of yours. With it, what the plan points to (files, figures, the text itself), read before anything is judged; what the plan is for, the outcome it is meant to bring about, in the target's or the user's words where they gave them and marked as your reading where they did not; and what the user has said that settles an assumption. Where several plans are on the table, the one the user is deciding on is the target and the others are named in a line. A question whether a claim is true, with no course of action in it, is not a plan, and that is said in a line.

What you do not know stays not known. It is not taken as absent, it is no reason to hold back what the evidence in hand supports, and it is not written as known into anything the user will say or use.

## What it does

List what the plan needs to be true in order to bring about what it is for: about the situation (demand, people, cost, time), about what others will do, and about what counts as success. Quote the target where it said one, and mark as your reading each one you supplied. What you supply is the centre of the work: an assumption the plan needs and nobody stated is now visible. A fact the plan leans on that rests on one source or on recall is an assumption too.

Keep the ones the decision turns on. Ask of each: if the opposite were true, would a different course be the right one? Those that pass are the key assumptions, seldom more than five; name in a line the ones you set aside. If none passes, say so, and that this does not show the plan is sound: none of the assumptions you found would change the decision if it were turned over.

Give each key assumption a contrary: not its bare denial but a specific state of affairs that could well be so ("the senior staff who live across town leave rather than move", not "the staff will not accept the move"). The contrary is your choice and is said to be. The contraries have to be able to hold together, and none may deny a fact both sides share, though it may read that fact differently, and then says how.

For each pair, say what observation would show which of the two is true, one the user could make, the cheapest first. Then settle what can be settled, from the material and from what the user has said: which of a pair is true, and whether the plan would still be the right course if the contrary were (work the plan's own figures under it). Where a tool can decide either, use it and say what ran; a sum you did not run is said to be worked by hand. Your recall settles nothing: neither side is given as fact a figure, a source or a quotation that the material or the user does not hold; what a contrary supposes or the counterplan chooses (a step, a date, a threshold) is said to be supposed or chosen; and a fact a side would need and nobody has is named as missing.

Where you put questions to the user, build the counterplan only once they have replied, or you were told that nobody will answer. It is the course of action the contraries still standing call for, on the same facts: a course a competent advocate would choose and defend, not the plan with a part removed and not a list of the plan's faults. Waiting, or doing nothing, counts where that is what follows. Give it the plan's own level of detail: where the plan names steps, costs and dates, so does the counterplan, and where it would need a figure nobody has, it says which. Say whether it is another way to what the plan is for or aims at something else. Where the contraries still standing no longer call for a different course (the evidence went the plan's way, or the plan holds under them), say that, build nothing, and judge.

Then judge. Say, pair by pair, which assumption the evidence supports, or that the pair is open; then, once the counterplan is built, which course you would take as things stand (the plan, the counterplan, or one built from the assumptions that survived on each side) and why. The plan left standing is as good a result as the plan overturned: the counterplan was built to test it, not to win. For what is open, say what would have to be seen, and by when, for the other course to be the right one.

## What it returns

The judgment first, in a few lines, as far as the evidence in hand settles it. Then what it rests on: what was taken as the plan and what it is for; the pairs, once each (the assumption, said by the target or supplied by you; its contrary; how the pair was settled, or what would decide it); the counterplan where one was built, whole; and what came to the surface and what did not, which is what the user or the material held that the plan did not use, what you could not know or check, with what each would change, and what was not examined at all (courses other than these two, whether the shared facts are accurate). A fact is given with how it is known: read or run here, supplied by the user, a single source, or your recall. Where the user needs a thing to act on, make it: the figures worked out, the check to make, the question to put to whoever holds the answer, the step changed.

You wrote both sides, and you say so: a counterplan built by the plan's examiner is not an independent test of the plan, and that both sides were heard is not evidence that the plan was tested. An examination of your own earlier recommendation is not independent either, and that is said in a line.

What only the user holds (what they meant, a preference, an authority, a fact no source holds) is asked at the end, all of it at once, each question naming the pair it would settle. A pair that waits on such an answer is open. Until the user has replied to those questions, the reply gives what the evidence in hand settles, the pairs and what would show which of each is true, and no counterplan, no choice between courses and no step for the user to take other than a check that would settle a pair, because what is handed over beside open questions gets used before the answers come. Once they have answered, or at once when told that nobody will answer, the counterplan is built with the contrary of each pair still open among those still standing (a pair their answer passed over or met with "I do not know", and every open pair when nobody will answer), and the judgment says what it turns on.

## When the user answers

A fact or a reason from the user that settles a pair settles it as far as it reaches, for either side, and what rested on the assumption that fell is built again. A contrary the user rejects for a defect in how it is put (it misstates a fact, it cannot hold with another contrary) is checked, and rewritten where the defect holds. Rejection alone, repetition or someone's standing leaves the pair as it stood: say so, and what would change it. The user's own account of their plan replaces your reading of it, but an assumption the plan cannot do without stays, and you show where the plan needs it; a plan they change is a new plan, examined again, with what the change gives up. To "I do not know", judge on what there is. A later reply gives what changed and the judgment as it now stands.
