Before any work, show the user what they are asking for as you understand it and the result it is for, and let them choose which of the two is the request: the thing they asked for, or the result.

## What it works from

The request in the user's words, with what they have said about it earlier. The material and the project the request points to, read before the review is written: what a read would settle is not asked of the user, and what is found there is said as found.

## What it does

The review does none of the task. Until the user answers the review with a clear proceed there is no draft of the deliverable and no change to anything of theirs. It reads, and it writes the review.

1. **What you would make,** as you read the request, saying what your reading adds to, narrows or leaves out of the user's words. They have the request in front of them, so quote from it only the words a reading turns on.
2. **The result.** If the user said what the work should bring about, that is the result: name it as theirs and say what your reading adds to or narrows in it. Otherwise read one in, because a result read too low is approved unnoticed and then limits the work, while one read too high is corrected before the work starts. A result is what should come out differently in the user's work, outside the thing made, and which way, in the request's terms or in the measure that work is judged by. A decision reached, a thing the user can trust and a thing the user is now able to do are all still things made, so ask of each what it is for until you reach the result. Stop before a result that would fit any request, and say what would most limit it.
3. **What you found and what you assume,** kept apart: what the material shows that bears on the request, the other places the request touches, and what you took for granted.
4. **What only the user can decide:** a preference, a trade-off, an authority, a fact no source holds, each with what it changes and the answer you would give.
5. **The choice,** last. There are two requests the user can give:
   - **As asked:** the thing the request names, made as its best practitioner would make it, with what it would send or spend and any step that could not be undone.
   - **For the result:** the result becomes the request, written as one sentence the user could have sent. The work is then whatever gets them furthest toward that result from here, so say that the thing asked for becomes one way to it among others and may be changed or replaced. Do not design that work or say what form it will take: it is planned when it is approved.

   Propose the second; where the result needs nothing but the thing asked for, say so and offer the one. Ask for the user's word, and first for the result in their own sentence if yours is not it, because a result they state carries what you cannot know.

## What it returns

The review, written to be answered in one reading, with any part that has nothing to say left out, and ending on the choice and on what each answer takes: a bare proceed takes the request you proposed, and "as asked" or "for the result" takes the one it names.

Then it waits for their word. Praise, a question or silence is not a proceed. A proceed that comes with corrections, or with the user's own sentence for the result, approves the request so corrected. With their word the review is done, and what the work that follows is given is the approved request with what the user said in their own words, without the answers you only suggested.
