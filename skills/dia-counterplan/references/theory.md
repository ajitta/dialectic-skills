# Dialectical inquiry: theory reference

The rules are in SKILL.md; this file says where they come from and where the skill departs from them. Everything is paraphrased from the sources at the end unless quoted. Mason's 1969 paper was read in abstract only; the procedure below comes from Schwenk's summary, so fidelity is medium-high and the procedure rests on secondary sources.

## 1. The method

- **Mason (1969).** Richard O. Mason proposed two criteria for a planning technique: it should expose the assumptions under a plan, and it "should suggest new and more relevant assumptions" (Mason, abstract). His case was a field study at RMK Abrasives.
- **The procedure, as Schwenk summarises it.** The plan's underlying assumptions (ten at RMK) were identified; a counterplan was built on opposite assumptions; the two were argued in a structured debate before management, after which the managers formed "a 'new, encompassing view'" (Schwenk, BEBR 814). This skill keeps the order (plan, assumptions, counterplan, synthesis). In place of the debate it settles each pair of assumptions from the material and from what the user has said, or leaves it open with what would decide it, and the synthesis is the assistant's own judgment of which course to take as things stand: the plan, the counterplan, or one built from the assumptions that survived on each side (SKILL.md, Your judgment).
- **A counterplan is a course of action.** Cosier: "dialectical inquiry requires specification of an alternative course of action" (Cosier 1981). Hence SKILL.md, The counterplan: the counterplan is a different plan, not the original with a part removed.
- **Lineage.** Mason's approach follows C. West Churchman's Hegelian inquiring system: Mitroff, Emshoff and Kilmann write of "the dialectical approach to strategic planning first suggested by Churchman … and Mason" (1979), and a later comparison says dialectical inquiry "derives from the work of C. West Churchman (1971), as further developed by R. O. Mason (1969)" (TQR 2006). Mitroff and Mason (1982) later formalised it in a way that "extends the work of Toulmin on argumentation".
- **Not Hegel's synthesis.** Hegel "never adopted Fichte's triadic 'thesis—antithesis—synthesis' language", and in his dialectic the movement comes from the content itself, with "nothing extraneous" introduced (SEP, "Hegel's Dialectics"). An assistant that writes the antithesis is such an outside party. So the skill does not call its result a Hegelian synthesis: a course built from the assumptions that survived on each side is a synthesis in Mason's sense of new and more relevant assumptions.

## 2. Which assumptions do the work (SAST)

Strategic Assumption Surfacing and Testing (SAST; Mason & Mitroff 1981) is a later variant of dialectical inquiry. Its test asks of each assumption: "If the opposite of this assumption is true, does it have any significant bearing on the issue?", and then rates each for importance and certainty (IfM SAST page). SKILL.md (The assumptions and their contraries) uses the test, narrowed to the decision ("if the opposite were true, would a different course be the right one?"), and omits the ratings to keep the output short.

## 3. Contraries, not negations

The negation of "demand grows 10%" is not a premise a plan can rest on; the assistant has to pick a contrary, and that choice is where bias enters (compare dia-reductio, which distinguishes contradictories from contraries). Schwenk summarises Mason's step as "the negation of these assumptions" and a counterplan "based on opposite assumptions" (BEBR 814); taken item by item, a bare negation gives a plan nothing to rest on, so the skill picks contraries instead (a departure, §7). Hence SKILL.md, The assumptions and their contraries: each contrary is a specific state of affairs that could well be so, said to be the assistant's choice, able to hold with the other contraries and denying no fact both sides share, so the user can see whose choice each is and name a defect in it.

## 4. Evidence on dialectical inquiry (DI) and devil's advocacy (DA)

All of these studies used human participants. In the group studies, separate subgroups or advocates developed the plan and the counterplan or critique; in the lab studies, participants received written reports presented as coming from different experts or planning committees. None tested a person who knows that one author wrote both sides.

- DI and DA both gave higher-quality recommendations and assumptions than consensus; DI beat DA on the quality of assumptions; consensus groups were more satisfied (Schweiger, Sandberg & Ragan 1986).
- In a longitudinal study, DI = DA > consensus on decision quality, with more re-evaluation of one's own assumptions and lower acceptance of the group decision (Schweiger, Sandberg & Rechner 1989).
- DI and DA groups exceeded the average of their members, and on several measures the best member (Schweiger & Sandberg 1989).
- Lab studies favoured DA over DI on performance. Schwenk also wrote that "Neither the DA nor the DIS was shown to be superior to the E in improving the quality of strategy statements", that "Proponents of the DIS may have overstated their case", and that "the DA should be recommended over the DIS". He explained the satisfaction gains by "a general belief … that when we have 'heard both sides' … we are better able to make a decision" (Schwenk, BEBR 814).
- A "carping critic" DA did not help where an objective DA did, and DA works only if decision-makers sincerely accept questioning. DA "may even help to solidify commitment to a disastrous course of action by giving the decision-makers confidence that they considered all points of view" (Schwenk 1984).
- Schwenk's 1989 meta-analysis, as given by an abstract aggregator (a single secondary source): "decision making is improved by the use of devil's advocacy or dialectical inquiry when the assumptions of an expert's report are incorrect, however neither method was proven to be superior."

What follows for the skill:

- **Why DI and not DA.** The evidence favours DA on performance and DI only on the quality of surfaced assumptions. This skill is DI anyway because its distinct output is the counterplan, which no sibling produces; the critic's role is already played by dia-quaestio's stress mode, which questions the user. The choice rests on that division of work, not on DI being better. There is no separate DA mode.
- **The false-assurance guard.** Schwenk's two warnings are why the reply states what was not examined and that both sides being heard is not evidence that the plan was tested, and why the counterplan must be a course of action and not a list of the plan's faults (SKILL.md, The counterplan and The reply).

## 5. One assistant in one chat

Fit for an AI chat is medium, not high.

- **No study tests one model writing both sides.** The format obliges a counter-case but cannot ensure its strength; assistants tend to match the user's views and people prefer such answers (Sharma et al. 2023), and across eleven models assistants affirmed users' actions about 50% more than humans did, while people trusted sycophantic models more (Cheng et al. 2025). The rule that the counterplan has the plan's own level of detail (SKILL.md, The counterplan) guards its strength, and the rule that rejection alone, repetition or someone's standing leaves a pair as it stood (The exchange) acts against yielding.
- **The nearest LLM studies are about groups.** Chiang et al. (2024) tested an LLM devil's advocate arguing against a separate model's advice in group decisions; the significant effect belonged to the interactive version ("helps groups significantly increase their appropriate reliance"), and the main effects were marginal (p = 0.064 and 0.063). Lee et al. (2025) found higher process and outcome satisfaction in 48 people in power-imbalanced groups; accuracy is not reported in the abstract. Neither studied one user, one-pass DI, or a model critiquing itself.
- **Self-examination.** Critiquing its own earlier recommendation is intrinsic self-correction, and "LLMs struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback" (Huang et al. 2024). So the assistant examines its own earlier recommendation only when the user points at it, and says that the one who made it is examining it.
- **Debate studies do not transfer.** Debate gains with human judges (Khan et al. 2024) came from separate debaters, a hidden source text and a quote verifier; a single chat has none of them, although the user can still act as judge. That is why the skill is named for Mason's method and not "debate".
- **The user's answers.** In DI the decision-maker judges; here the assistant gives its own judgment, and the user's answers move it by what they carry. A fact or a reason that settles a pair settles it as far as it reaches, for either side; a contrary the user rejects is rewritten where a defect in how it is put holds (it misstates a fact, it cannot hold with another contrary), and rejection alone, repetition or someone's standing leaves the pair as it stood. The user's own account of their plan replaces the assistant's reading of it, but an assumption the plan cannot do without stays, and the assistant shows where the plan needs it (SKILL.md, The exchange).

## 6. From the Talmud: the open question and the minority view

Two Talmudic practices bear on the skill; neither comes from Mason:

- **The open pair (*teyku*).** A Talmudic problem with two balanced sides and no deciding authority ends *teyku*, "let it stand", unsolved; there are more than 300 such cases in the Babylonian Talmud (Jacobs). The skill likewise says that a key pair is open when the evidence does not settle it, with what would decide it, instead of forcing a choice (SKILL.md, The assumptions and their contraries, and Your judgment). Unlike *teyku*, which Jacobs calls inherently insoluble, an open pair here is open only for now; the parallel is the refusal to force a choice, not a claim that the pair cannot be decided.
- **The way back to the side set aside.** The Mishnah records a minority opinion so that a later court may rely on it (Mishnah Eduyot 1:5). The skill keeps no written argument for the side set aside; the nearest it comes is to say, for what is open, what would have to be seen, and by when, for the other course to be the right one, and what would change its judgment (SKILL.md, Your judgment). Eduyot 1:6 gives a rival reason (Rabbi Judah: the minority view is recorded so it can be identified as set aside).

## 7. Departures, in one list

- One assistant restates the plan and writes the counterplan; in Mason's study the plan and counterplan had different authors and were debated before a group of managers.
- The assistant writes the synthesis: it settles each pair from the evidence or leaves it open, and says which course it would take as things stand; in Mason's study the managers formed the new view after the debate.
- Contraries are chosen by the assistant and labelled, not presented as the opposition's own view.
- Contraries replace the negated ("opposite") assumptions of Schwenk's summary.
- SAST's test is used without its importance and certainty ratings.
- Leaving a pair open, with what would decide it, has a parallel in *teyku* (§6).
