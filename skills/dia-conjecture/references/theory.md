# Proofs and refutations: theory reference

The rules are in SKILL.md; this file says where they come from and where the skill departs from Lakatos. Sources are listed at the end; everything here is paraphrased from them unless quoted.

## 1. The method

- Imre Lakatos's *Proofs and Refutations* (British Journal for the Philosophy of Science, 1963-64; book 1976, edited by Worrall and Zahar) is a classroom dialogue on Euler's conjecture that V − E + F = 2 for polyhedra. Chapter 1 names the moves this skill uses (CUP contents).
- **Proof:** a "thought experiment" that decomposes the conjecture into lemmas (SEP). Listing the lemmas is proof-analysis.
- **Local and global counterexamples:** a local counterexample "refutes a lemma (without necessarily refuting the main conjecture)"; a global one "refutes the main conjecture itself" (*Proofs and Refutations* I, BJPS 1963).
- **The combinations** (SEP): global but not local, unearth the "hidden lemma"; local but not global, replace the lemma; both, incorporate the lemma as a condition.

## 2. Rules 1-4

As quoted by Easwaran (Rule 4 in his note 5):

- **Rule 1.** "If you have a conjecture, set out to prove it and to refute it. Inspect the proof carefully to prepare a list of non-trivial lemmas (proof-analysis); find counterexamples both to the conjecture (global counterexamples) and to the suspect lemmas (local counterexamples)."
- **Rule 2.** "If you have a global counterexample discard your conjecture, add to your proof-analysis a suitable lemma that will be refuted by it, and replace the discarded conjecture by an improved one that incorporates that lemma as a condition. Do not allow a refutation to be dismissed as a monster. Make all 'hidden lemmas' explicit."
- **Rule 3.** "If you have a local counterexample, check to see whether it is not also a global counterexample."
- **Rule 4.** For a local but not global counterexample, the proof is improved by "replacing the refuted lemma by an unfalsified one"; the conjecture stands.

The skill applies Rules 1 and 3: the argument is set out in steps, and each case's check says what it breaks. The repair applies Rules 2 and 4.

## 3. The responses Lakatos rejects

Chapter 1 section 4 ("Criticism of the conjecture by global counterexamples") names surrender, monster-barring, exception-barring, monster-adjustment and lemma-incorporation (CUP contents).

- **Monster-barring** claims the counterexample "never was a member of the domain"; **exception-barring** shrinks the domain to exclude it (Easwaran). Easwaran calls these "unproductive responses to counterexamples that he rejects". The skill counts neither as a repair and names them when they appear: listing the case as an exception explains nothing and invites the next one, and redefining a term so that the case is no longer an A makes a different, narrower claim, said as that with what it gives up. Monster-adjustment (ch. 1 section 4d) is not in Easwaran's list, and the skill does not name it.
- Restriction without explanation risks "an empty tautology" (SEP), as in "For all Eulerian polyhedra … V − E + F = 2".
- The Teacher refuses "surreptitious changes in my position by sentences starting with 'I meant …'" (*Proofs and Refutations* I). The skill follows it: "by A I meant ...", where it narrows the source's own words and not wording the assistant added or changed, changes the claim, and the old wording and the new are both said.
- **Surrender** is voiced by the "Popperian" student Gamma (SEP). The skill keeps it: giving the claim up is a result as well.

## 4. What is implemented, and what is not

- **Implemented:** chapter 1 sections 1-5 and Rules 1-4. Section 6, "Increasing content by deeper proofs", is the content-increasing half of the method; SEP calls it "a deeper problem than finding the domain of validity". The skill does not use its deeper-proof move (6a): a case that breaks only a step calls for a better step and leaves the claim as it is. For a case that counts against the claim, the skill builds in the weakest condition that shuts the case out and still lets the argument through; that is the skill's reading of Rule 2's "a suitable lemma", not section 6. So a repair restricts the claim no more than the case requires.
- **Not implemented:** section 7 (Rule 5 and "deductive guessing versus naive guessing", "increasing content by deductive guessing"), and the concept-stretching analysis of sections 8 ("Refutation by concept-stretching. A reappraisal of monster-barring") and 9 ("Unlimited concept-stretching destroys meaning and truth") (CUP contents). In those sections Lakatos partly re-legitimises monster-barring against counterexamples that stretch a concept; SEP asks whether there is "any distinction to be drawn between interesting and frivolous concept-stretching". This skill does not take up that analysis: a case that meets the claim only under a reading wider than the claim's does not count against it unless the user adopts the wider reading, which is a changed claim against which the case is checked again; and a narrowing of the source's own words changes the claim, while a correction that only brings the assistant's restatement back to those words does not.

## 5. Departures from Lakatos (labelled)

- **Domain:** Lakatos wrote about informal mathematics. The skill takes any universal claim (all, always, never, none): a rule, a generalisation or a property said to hold of every case. A claim put as "usually" or "most" is not refuted by a case and is not tested.
- **Roles:** Lakatos's class of many students is compressed into one assistant, which raises the cases, says whether each counts wherever its check settles that, and builds the repair, and the user, who owns the claim and can name a defect in a case; in the book the class argues it out.
- **Reconstructed proofs:** when no reason was given, the assistant reconstructs one and so writes the proof it attacks, that is, it argues both sides. The skill says so.
- **Monster-barring:** Rule 2 forbids it. The skill treats a narrowing of a term in the source's own words as a change of the claim, and a correction that only brings the assistant's restatement back to those words as none: for a change it says the old wording and the new and what the new one no longer covers, and tests the new claim.
- **Answers to a case:** the skill weighs the user's answer to a case by the defect it names: one that holds (a condition of the check that fails, a term read wider than the claim reads it, a fact the assistant lacked) withdraws the case or leaves it standing against a step only, and disagreement alone, repetition or someone's standing moves nothing. This is the skill's own, not Lakatos's.
- **Popper's prior refutation criterion; an addition to Lakatos.** For a claim about the world, the skill says before the cases what observation would count against it, so that a case is not explained away once it is found. Popper: "criteria of refutation have to be laid down beforehand: it must be agreed which observable situations, if actually observed, mean that the theory is refuted" (*Conjectures and Refutations* 38 n.3, via SEP "Karl Popper"). Lakatos himself "flatly denies that there are critical tests, in the Popperian sense" (same entry), so this is not his method.
- **What every case breaks:** Lakatos's Rule 3 checks a local counterexample for being global too; the skill applies the check to every case and says what each breaks (the claim, a step of the argument, or what is built on the claim), because the repair differs by what is broken. Checking what is built on the claim is the skill's addition.
- **The judgment** is the skill's own: whether the claim stands, which means holding against the cases tried and never proved, with the cases and kinds of case tried and not tried, and what becomes of what rested on the claim.

## 6. Why each case carries its check

The reason is a risk finding, not support for the method. In *The Counterexample Game* (Drucker and Mahowald, 2026), one model instance proposed counterexamples to a definition, another repaired it, and the loop repeated:

- on a sample of 60 counterexamples, five human raters together judged 32% valid, while the model judge rated 60% valid; the judge accepts roughly twice as many as humans do;
- validity fell from 69% to 17-30% over a chain of counterexamples and repairs, as rated by the model; the authors say these figures are "probably best read as upper bounds";
- repairs became "a laundry list of exceptions", which is exception-barring.

The setup was model against model, on definitions, with no human in the loop. So each case shows its check, condition by condition, so that the user can fault it; a case whose check cannot be written is not raised; what follows from a case that rests on recall is conditional on it; a repair is put to the cases again, and to a new case aimed at the condition added, before it is offered; and when conditions pile up one by one, the skill looks for the one condition behind them. No study has tested this method with users; studies of AI that asks questions or plays devil's advocate (Danry et al.; Chiang et al.) used different setups and are distant analogues at best.

## 7. Lineage

- SEP: Lakatos thought "Hegelian language" could describe the growth of mathematics (*Proofs and Refutations*, p. 146). IEP: "Lakatos extended the range of critical rationalism into mathematics".
- Pease et al. formalised the same moves as a dialogue game.
