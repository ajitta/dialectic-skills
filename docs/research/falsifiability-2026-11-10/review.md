# Review of the falsifiability record (2026-11-11)

Status: a review of this folder by the agent that maintains the plugin, written before any decision. It re-counts the grade sheets, reads all 36 transcripts and both relay logs, checks every claim about skill text against `skills/` at 2.11.0, checks the philosophy claims against primary text where it could be fetched (Popper's *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*; other works through the Stanford Encyclopedia), and re-runs the routing on a second harness (21 runs, section 3). An independent reviewer (a separate agent session, read-only) checked the draft: "sound with fixes"; its four blockers were two places where the basis for a verdict left out evidence pointing the other way, a pass rule described as written before runs it was written after, and a run described as answering when it had only classified. All are fixed below. Where this review and the record differ, the review wins. Nothing here changes a skill.

Terms, as the record uses them:

- `dia-x:N` is `skills/dia-x/SKILL.md` line N. Paths without a folder are in this folder. Run names (C1r3, B3r2) are files in `exp2/t/`; "v1 C1" is `exp1/t/C1.md`.
- Arms: A, a plain answer with no skill; B, dia with the patch `exp1/light-fix-B.patch`; C, dia 2.11.0 unchanged. Inputs: I1, a drug claim (statistical); I2, a market claim with no time bound; I3, a "hidden pattern" claim (existence).
- Scoring items (`exp2/scoring.md`): K1, the claim's form is classified correctly; K2, a decision rule is asked for or its need stated (I1); K3, conditions to hold fixed are named (I1, I3); K4, a bounded version is treated as a changed claim and the refusal of every criterion is named (I2); K5, no verdict on truth; K6, no criterion recorded as agreed unless the user agreed.
- v1: 9 dialogues, 1 run per cell, one grader; its bar was K1 met on all three inputs and at least 11 of 13 points. v2: 27 dialogues, 3 runs per cell, two blind graders scoring independently, called "opus" and "fable" after their models (Claude Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2); its bar was K1 met in at least 8 of an arm's 9 runs and at least 33 of 39 points.
- The simulated user answered by fixed rules: persona lines for each input, and generic replies ("the recommended option, else the first", "not sure") otherwise. The orchestrator chose which rule applied and relayed the reply.

## 1. What holds

- **The numbers.** Every count in both result files and in `README.md` reproduces from the grade sheets and keys: the bar table, the per-run table, the routing column, 10 disagreements in 116 items scored by both graders, and v1's 11, 13 and 12 of 13. All 10 disagreements are on items both graders flagged as borderline.
- **The data handling.** `mkblind.py` with its seed rebuilds `key.txt` and 26 of 27 blinded files byte for byte; the 27th differs by the one hand edit the log names. All 126 relayed replies in v2 match `exp2/relay-log.md`, and the wrap-up came after the fifth assistant message in 25 of 25 full-length runs.
- **The quotations.** A script written for this review found 91 quoted spans of skill text in `analysis.md` and `skill-need.md`: 87 verbatim, 4 differing by markup or light paraphrase. The patch applies cleanly to 2.11.0.
- **The verification step.** All eleven findings it made on `skill-need.md`, and its four split rulings, hold against the skill text.
- **The philosophy.** Popper's text supports each claim the record uses: strictly existential statements cannot be falsified (*The Logic of Scientific Discovery* §15); probability statements are not falsifiable and are rejected only by a methodological decision (§65-66); auxiliary hypotheses are acceptable when they raise testability (§20); the criteria of refutation are laid down beforehand (as quoted in the Stanford Encyclopedia entry on Popper).
- **The record's own candour.** It names the grader dependence, the author's bias in arm B, the different loading of arms B and C, and the first-option rule's effect on C1r3 and C2 (`experiment-v2-result.md`, conclusion and "How far to trust this"; `README.md`, Limits).
- **The main conclusion: no new skill.** Under every grader at least one existing path met the bar. Section 3 adds that on a second harness no request was sent to a skill that then failed to respond to it; 2 of 21 asked which of two skills to run.

## 2. Corrections

1. **The repository had already considered this, and the record cites none of it.** The candidate research of 2026-10-21 did not recommend "Popper as a skill" and put the prior refutation criterion into dia-conjecture (`docs/research/candidates/README.md`, "Not recommended"); the owner's picks followed it (spec D35). Spec D57 removed the router's per-skill border bullets and kept "those two border sentences and nothing else", each of which closed a measured miss (0 of 5 and 2 of 5 right before, 10 of 10 after). `docs/probes.md` Part F measured dia-quaestio's stress mode on another subject: the falsification question was asked in 1 of 2 sessions, and the other brief listed it under "Not tested". The record says it did not read `docs/research/`.
2. **"A statistical claim, fixed by conjecture as a strict universal" (`README.md`, Gaps) is mostly the simulated user's doing**, as `experiment-v2-result.md` already says under bias; the Gaps list should match. dia-conjecture asked whether "every" is meant strictly, as dia-conjecture:39 requires. Both runs listed the strict reading first. C1r2 ended by recasting the choice as a yes-or-no question (would one patient without effect make you withdraw it?), which was relayed as "what would count against it" and got the persona line ("if there is no difference from placebo"). C1r3 ended on the choice and got "1", the first option (`exp2/relay-log.md` lines 22 and 25). C1r2 then met K1 under both graders and C1r3 lost K1 and K2 under both. Arm C's pass under the fable grader (K1 8 of 9, the bar exactly) depends on which of the two replies a run got.
3. **"An existential claim … was declined at the first reply" is accurate, and "no destination" is not what failed.** C3r1 named the claim's form (an existence claim that no single counterexample breaks) and pointed to dia-quaestio, from the existing Step 0 line for an idea (dia-conjecture:35). It did not say whether the claim is falsifiable. The run was then ended after one message as "closing" (correction 6).
4. **The predicted failure of dia-conjecture on an unbounded claim did not happen in v2.** `verification-result.md` lists as a remaining gap that hard rule 2 would leave no case to raise, so the run would end "stopped before testing" and hide the finding. The label appeared once, in v1 C2, after v1's wrap-up at the third message, and that run still stated the finding. In v2 all 3 dia-conjecture runs on the market claim raised a checkable case (a mispriced thing that ceased to exist before any correction), recorded the holder's reply in the existing Prior criterion field, said that under that criterion no observation can refute the claim, and ended "open". The v1 result notes the failed prediction; the v2 result, `README.md` and `verification-result.md` do not.
5. **The key for the market claim is one defensible answer, not the only one.** `exp2/scoring.md` scores "unfalsifiable by form". Popper puts the unfalsifiability of a "for every x there is a y" statement on the unbounded "there is", and says a bounded one "may be falsifiable" (§66, note *2). A mispricing whose asset is delisted or liquidated is bounded by the asset's life, so "it ended uncorrected" can count against the claim, provided the mispricing is identified independently of later prices (the twin-share case in C2r1 is). That last step is this review's inference from the note, not a sourced statement about markets. The opus grader gave K1 = 0 to C2r1 and C2r3 for that answer; the fable grader gave 1. Counting both as met leaves arm C at 7 of 9 under opus, still below the bar, so no bar verdict changes.
6. **Three relay choices treated the dia arms differently from arm A; the result names one of them.** That they favoured arm A is this review's reading, not a measurement.
   - The definition of "pattern" reached arm A in 3 of 3 v2 runs, because an invitation to name the pattern was treated as the question. It reached the dia-quaestio runs in 0 of 5, because their restatement ("is that right?") got "not sure". K3 for that input is scored "after definition". (Arm A earned K1 in its first reply, before the definition.)
   - A message that asked nothing ended the dialogue in arm C twice (C3r1, C1r2, both a stop and redirect by the skill) and was answered "not sure" and continued in arm A (for example A2r1). `exp2/protocol.md` ends a dialogue on a "closing summary" without defining one, so the fault is the protocol's.
   - The strict-or-mostly question was answered "not sure" in v1 and with the first option in v2, though `exp2/protocol.md` says the user rules are unchanged. The result names this one.
7. **Arm A's pass under the fable grader rests on one disputed item.** A scores 33, the bar exactly; A3r2 K6 is 1 for fable and 0 for opus, both flagged borderline. On arm A's other four disputed items (K5 on A2r1, A2r2, A2r3 and A3r1) the fable grader scored against A, three times for a remark on where the evidence leans for a reworded claim (A2r1, A2r2, A2r3) and once for calling the original claim trivially true (A3r1). The same grader model read the reworded-claim case the other way in v1; under the v2 reading v1's arm A would score 10 of 13 and miss the v1 bar.
8. **No run exercised either dia-conjecture hunk, and arm B's K3 was partly earned by a patch-only field.** All 12 arm B runs went to dia-quaestio. Arm B earned K3 for a field that exists only in the patch and read "conditions to hold fixed: not asked" (B3r3). The record's Limits already say that arm B read a scratch copy by file path while arm C used the Skill tool; this review adds that what stood in for "your skill list" (dia-router:57) in arm B is not recorded.
9. **Smaller points.**
   - `experiment-v2-result.md` says the C2 runs recorded "the user refused every deadline"; no deadline was offered in those runs. The recorded criterion is "only not yet corrected, not a counterexample".
   - It says both graders faulted B3r2; only the fable grader did.

## 5. What this review proposes instead

Nothing in the record or in section 3 supports a change to a skill file. Three changes to the repository's own documents follow from the review; each is the owner's decision.

1. **Rules for comparisons with a simulated user** (recommended), as rule 6 of `docs/writing-for-models/README.md` § Rules for A/B records (spec D53), with one line in `AGENTS.md` § Probes and evals pointing there, because this record sat outside that folder. Each rule answers a defect in section 2:
   - For a dialogue skill, prefer the adaptive respondent the repository already has (a model playing a persona that answers the questions; `docs/skill-effect.md`, "Re-run after D27, and with an adaptive respondent"). When replies are fixed instead, write their order in the protocol: the persona's own line first, a generic reply ("first option", "not sure") only when no persona line answers the question; the same question gets the same reply in every arm and run (corrections 2 and 6).
   - One ending rule, defined in the protocol and applied the same way in every arm (correction 6).
   - Start every arm through the same loading mechanism, so that the text under test is the only difference (the record's own Limits; correction 8).
   - Check an answer key that rests on a named theory against a primary source before the runs (correction 5; this extends rule 4, judge keys).
   - Before proposing a new skill or a router sentence, read the spec decision rows and `docs/research/candidates/` for earlier work on it (correction 1).
2. **Merge this folder to `main` as documents only**, with this review as its correction layer (recommended). No skill file, manifest or generated output changes, so there is no version bump.
3. **Two stale lines in the repository `README.md` file list** (low priority). `docs/skill-effect.md` and `docs/token-cost.md` are described as covering "the seven 1.2.x skills"; the first now runs through Round 11 (2026-11-05) and compares every skill except dia-techne and dia-router with a plain request, and the second runs through plugin 2.3.0 (spec D59). A reader looking for the plugin's evidence is undersold, as `analysis.md` was. Fix with the next README change, in the three translations too.

## 6. If a router sentence is wanted later

The test that would justify one: a request cell where the base build makes a harmful pick (section 3's test, which is this review's and not spec D57's) in 3 or more of 5 runs; then the sentence at 5 runs on that cell, plus one control cell per neighbour it could pull from (a strictly universal claim that must still reach dia-conjecture; a plan that must still reach dia-counterplan or dia-quaestio; a single-claim question that must still get "none fits"). No such cell exists today. Real-use transcripts where a falsifiability request was badly served would be the place to find one.
