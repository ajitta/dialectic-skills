# Halden, M. (2009). "Fixed real withdrawals from mixed portfolios." Norland Journal of Retirement Finance 14(2): 101–131.

Abstract. Using Norland market returns from 1926 to 2005, we test fixed real withdrawals over a 30-year retirement. Portfolios held between 50% and 75% in stocks, the rest in government bonds. An initial withdrawal of 4% of the starting balance, raised each year by the actual change in the household price index (HPI), lasted at least 33 years in every historical period tested, and 50 years or more in most periods. A 3% withdrawal lasted at least 50 years in every period.

Results (p. 118). Among the allocations tested, a 50/50 mix was near-optimum for portfolio longevity. For retirees who also want to leave a larger estate, we suggest an allocation close to 75% stocks. In the worst starting period, a 5% withdrawal was exhausted after 20 years.

Limitations (p. 127). The results rest on Norland data and a 30-year horizon. They say nothing about other countries, about horizons beyond 30 years, or about portfolios outside the 50–75% stock range.
