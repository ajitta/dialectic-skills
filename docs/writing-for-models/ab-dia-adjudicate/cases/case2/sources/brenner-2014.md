# Brenner, I. (2014). "Induced traffic in Ostmark cities." Ostmark Transport Review 9(3): 44–79.

Summary. We studied 40 cities in the Ostmark region between 1995 and 2010. Adding one lane-kilometre of urban highway was followed by a roughly proportional rise in vehicle-kilometres travelled within five years; the estimated elasticity was about 0.9. The study covered urban highways only; rural roads, bus lanes and rail were not studied.

Findings (p. 61). In the first year after a new lane opened, traffic on parallel streets fell by about 10%, before rising again. In cities that also priced congestion, the five-year rise was about half as large.

Note (p. 72). The elasticity is an average across cities; individual cities ranged from 0.6 to 1.1.
