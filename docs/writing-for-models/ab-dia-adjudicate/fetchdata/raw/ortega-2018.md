Ortega, L. (2018). Street trees and summer heat in Velland. Velland Journal of Urban Climate 6(1): 12–40.
Summary. We measured afternoon air temperature on 120 streets in three Velland cities in July and August of 2015–2017. Streets with tree canopy above 40% were on average 2.1 °C cooler than streets with canopy below 10%. Cooling was largest on narrow residential streets and smallest on wide arterial roads.
Results (p. 25). Each 10-point rise in canopy cover was associated with 0.5 °C lower afternoon air temperature. At night the difference shrank to about 0.4 °C. Surface temperatures of pavement fell by up to 12 °C under dense canopy.
Population note (p. 31). The streets studied were in temperate cities; the study included no arid or tropical sites. Health outcomes were not measured.
Limitations (p. 36). Canopy cover and street width were correlated, so part of the cooling may reflect street width rather than trees.
