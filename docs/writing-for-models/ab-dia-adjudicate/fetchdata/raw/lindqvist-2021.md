Lindqvist, K. (2021). Pair programming in practice. Nordvik Software Engineering Review 11(4): 201–230.
Summary. Across 14 teams at 5 Nordvik firms over 18 months, tasks done in pairs took 15% more person-hours than tasks done alone, but had 40% fewer defects found after release. Teams new to pairing saw the extra effort fall from 30% in the first two months to about 10% after six months.
Results (p. 214). The defect reduction was largest for tasks rated complex and close to zero for routine tasks. Developers reported higher satisfaction in pairs, though satisfaction was measured only in the final month.
Scope (p. 226). All teams built business web applications; no embedded or safety-critical software was studied. Remote pairing was not studied.
